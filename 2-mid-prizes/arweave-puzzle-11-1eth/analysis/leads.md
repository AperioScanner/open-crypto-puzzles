# Open leads, ranked

## 1. CryptoCanvas post-publication mirror provenance (active, closing)

Stage 89 establishes that CryptoCanvas token #5 was minted on **2020-07-24**, roughly three months after Puzzle #11 was published. CryptoCanvas is therefore **not a pre-publication source**.

The remaining bounded question is whether token #5 actually mirrored Puzzle #11, as reported by a 2021 reverse-image search. Stage 89 only recovered an OpenSea opengraph card, not the original NFT asset.

Stage 90 attempts to recover cached original NFT media/metadata from Blockscout, current OpenSea embedded data, and historical Internet Archive indexes, while also recording deployment/mint provenance.

## 2. Format-invariant visible/semantic mechanisms

Still the strongest broad prior. Stage 31 showed visible structure survives JPEG/resampling while exact low-bit identity collapses near chance.

## 3. Public-address known-answer probe

The author explicitly said the public address is present somewhere in the image/file. Equal-width bit/hex mappings were rejected; arbitrary tilings remain disfavored.

## 4. External archival evidence / sibling solve methods

Still potentially the best route once the CryptoCanvas branch is closed.

## 5. H/V skyline texture — secondary

Real and reproducible, but every attempted semantic/selector/reflection interpretation has failed independent confirmation.

## 6. Superbatch nominal hits — retired

Stage 88 independently rejected A11-EXP-043, A11-EXP-050 and A11-EXP-061.

Broad low-bit, traversal, alpha, ASCII/compression and generic statistical searches have already been covered or explained by ordinary image structure.
